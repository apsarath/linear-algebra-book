---
title: The Distance Between Two Vectors
---


We said that $\vv{b} - \vv{a}$ is the displacement from $\vv{a}$ to $\vv{b}$. The *distance* between the two (thought of as points) is just the length of that displacement. Going the other way, $\vv{a} - \vv{b} = -(\vv{b} - \vv{a})$ has the same length, so the order doesn't matter, and we usually write:

$$
d(\vv{a}, \vv{b}) = \norm{\vv{a} - \vv{b}}.
$$

<Example>

The distance between $\vv{a} = \colvec{1\\3}$ and $\vv{b} = \colvec{4\\-1}$ is

$$
\norm{\vv{a} - \vv{b}} = \norm{\colvec{-3\\4}} = \sqrt{9+16} = 5.
$$

</Example>

<Exercise>

Find the distance between $\vv{u} = \colvec{2\\-1\\3}$ and $\vv{v} = \colvec{0\\3\\1}$.

</Exercise>

<Solution>

$\vv{u} - \vv{v} = \colvec{2\\-4\\2}$, so $d(\vv{u},\vv{v}) = \norm{\colvec{2\\-4\\2}} = \sqrt{4+16+4} = \sqrt{24} = 2\sqrt{6}$.

</Solution>
