---
title: 'Vectors in $\R^n$'
---


Everything we've done generalizes effortlessly from $\R^2$ and $\R^3$ to $\R^n$. A vector in $\R^n$ is an ordered list of $n$ real numbers:

$$
\vv{a} = \colvec{a_1 \\ a_2 \\ \vdots \\ a_n}.
$$

We define:

- **Addition:** $\vv{a} + \vv{b} = \colvec{a_1 + b_1 \\ a_2 + b_2 \\ \vdots \\ a_n + b_n}$
- **Scalar multiplication:** $c\,\vv{a} = \colvec{ca_1 \\ ca_2 \\ \vdots \\ ca_n}$
- **Magnitude:** $\norm{\vv{a}} = \sqrt{a_1^2 + a_2^2 + \cdots + a_n^2}$

As we noted when we first met $\R^n$, we can't draw pictures when $n > 3$, but every formula above works exactly as it does in the plane. This is one of the great strengths of linear algebra: geometric intuition developed in two and three dimensions guides our reasoning in higher dimensions, even when we can't visualize what's happening.
