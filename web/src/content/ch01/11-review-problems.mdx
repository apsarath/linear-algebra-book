<Exercise id="rev-computation-warmup" title="Computation Warm-up" solution="appendix">

Let $\vv{a} = \colvec{2\\-5\\1}$ and $\vv{b} = \colvec{-3\\4\\2}$. Compute each of the following.

1. $\vv{a} + \vv{b}$
2. $3\vv{a} - 2\vv{b}$
3. $\norm{\vv{a}}$
4. $\norm{\vv{a} - \vv{b}}$
5. A unit vector in the direction of $\vv{b}$

</Exercise>

<Exercise id="rev-vector-arithmetic" title="Vector Arithmetic" solution="appendix">

Let $\vv{p} = \colvec{1\\-2\\3\\0}$ and $\vv{q} = \colvec{-1\\4\\1\\-3}$. Compute:

1. $\vv{p} + \vv{q}$
2. $\norm{\vv{p}}$
3. $d(\vv{p}, \vv{q})$
4. $\frac{1}{2}(\vv{p} + \vv{q})$ — what point does this represent geometrically?

</Exercise>

<Exercise id="rev-true-false" title="True or False" solution="appendix">

True or false. If false, give a counterexample.

1. For any vectors $\vv{u}$ and $\vv{v}$ in $\R^n$, $\norm{\vv{u} + \vv{v}} = \norm{\vv{u}} + \norm{\vv{v}}$.
2. For any scalar $c$ and vector $\vv{v}$ in $\R^n$, $\norm{c\,\vv{v}} = c\,\norm{\vv{v}}$.
3. The zero vector $\vv{0}$ is a linear combination of any set of vectors.
4. If $\vv{u} + \vv{v} = \vv{u} + \vv{w}$, then $\vv{v} = \vv{w}$.

</Exercise>

<Exercise id="rev-lincomb-hunt" title="Linear Combination Hunt" solution="appendix">

Let $\vv{v}_1 = \colvec{1\\2}$ and $\vv{v}_2 = \colvec{3\\-1}$. For each of the following vectors, either express it as a linear combination of $\vv{v}_1$ and $\vv{v}_2$, or explain why this is impossible.

1. $\colvec{7\\5}$
2. $\colvec{0\\0}$
3. $\colvec{1\\1}$

</Exercise>

<Exercise id="rev-midpoints" title="Midpoints and Centroids" solution="appendix">

Let $P = \colvec{1\\3}$, $Q = \colvec{5\\-1}$, and $R = \colvec{-2\\4}$ be three points in $\R^2$ (represented as position vectors).

1. Find the midpoint of segment $\overline{PQ}$ using the formula $\frac{1}{2}(\vv{p} + \vv{q})$.
2. The **centroid** of triangle $PQR$ is $\frac{1}{3}(\vv{p}+\vv{q}+\vv{r})$. Compute it.
3. Explain why both the midpoint formula and the centroid formula are linear combinations. What are the weights?

</Exercise>

</Exercise>

<Exercise id="rev-distance-puzzle" title="Distance Puzzle" solution="appendix">

Three points in $\R^2$ are $A = (1,1)$, $B = (4,5)$, and $C = (7,1)$. Compute $\norm{\overrightarrow{AB}}$, $\norm{\overrightarrow{BC}}$, and $\norm{\overrightarrow{AC}}$. Is triangle $ABC$ equilateral, isosceles, or scalene?

</Exercise>

<Exercise id="rev-collinearity" title="Collinearity Test" solution="appendix">

Points $P$, $Q$, $R$ in $\R^2$ are *collinear* (lie on the same line) if and only if $\overrightarrow{PR} = t\,\overrightarrow{PQ}$ for some scalar $t$. Determine whether the following three points are collinear: $P = (1,2)$, $Q = (3,5)$, $R = (7,11)$.

</Exercise>

<Exercise id="rev-unit-square" title="The Unit Square" solution="appendix">

The four corners of the unit square in $\R^2$ are $\vv{0}$, $\vv{e}_1$, $\vv{e}_2$, and $\vv{e}_1 + \vv{e}_2$. What is the length of the diagonal from $\vv{0}$ to $\vv{e}_1 + \vv{e}_2$? Generalize: what is the length of the “main diagonal” of the unit hypercube in $\R^n$ (from $\vv{0}$ to $\vv{e}_1 + \vv{e}_2 + \cdots + \vv{e}_n$)?

</Exercise>

<Exercise id="rev-weighted-averages" title="Weighted Averages" solution="appendix">

A hiker's GPS records three waypoints as position vectors: $\vv{p}_1 = \colvec{2\\5}$, $\vv{p}_2 = \colvec{6\\3}$, $\vv{p}_3 = \colvec{4\\-1}$. She spent 1 hour at the first, 2 hours at the second, and 1 hour at the third. Her “time-weighted average position” is

$$
\frac{1 \cdot \vv{p}_1 + 2 \cdot \vv{p}_2 + 1 \cdot \vv{p}_3}{1+2+1}.
$$

Compute it. Why is this a linear combination (and what are the weights)?

</Exercise>

<Exercise id="rev-parallelogram-area" title="Parallelogram Area" solution="appendix">

Two adjacent sides of a parallelogram are $\vv{u} = \colvec{3\\0}$ and $\vv{v} = \colvec{1\\4}$. List the four vertices (with one vertex at the origin) and find the area of the parallelogram.

<Hint>

One side lies on the $x$-axis, so the height is easy to read off. Area of a parallelogram is base times height.

</Hint>

</Exercise>

<Exercise id="rev-multiple-equations" title="Multiple Vector Equations" solution="appendix">

\vv{x} + \colvec{2\\3} = 3\colvec{1\\-1} \qquad\text{and}\qquad \norm{\vv{x}} = \sqrt{26}.

</Exercise>

<Exercise id="rev-norm-properties" title="Norm Properties" solution="appendix">

Let $\vv{u}$ and $\vv{v}$ be vectors in $\R^n$. Show algebraically (by expanding in components) that

$$
\norm{\vv{u}+\vv{v}}^2 = \norm{\vv{u}}^2 + 2\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_i v_i + \norm{\vv{v}}^2.
$$

</Exercise>

<Exercise id="rev-parallelogram-law" title="Parallelogram Law" solution="appendix">

Using the result of the previous exercise, prove the **parallelogram law**: for any $\vv{a}, \vv{b} \in \R^n$,

$$
\norm{\vv{a} + \vv{b}}^2 + \norm{\vv{a} - \vv{b}}^2 = 2\norm{\vv{a}}^2 + 2\norm{\vv{b}}^2.
$$

Give a geometric interpretation for $n = 2$: what do the two sides of the equation measure about a parallelogram?

</Exercise>

<Exercise id="rev-nearest-point" title="Nearest Point on a Line" solution="appendix">

Let $\vv{v} = \colvec{1\\2}$. Among all scalar multiples $t\vv{v}$ (with $t \in \R$), which one is closest to the point $\vv{p} = \colvec{5\\1}$? Find the value of $t$ that minimizes $\norm{\vv{p} - t\vv{v}}$.

<Hint>

Minimize $\norm{\vv{p}-t\vv{v}}^2$ as a function of $t$; it's a quadratic.

</Hint>

</Exercise>

<Exercise id="rev-clock" title="Clock Vectors" solution="appendix">

The tip of the minute hand of a clock lies at $\colvec{0\\5}$ (pointing at 12). After 15 minutes (a quarter turn clockwise), it points at $\colvec{5\\0}$ (pointing at 3). What is the displacement vector (change in position) of the tip of the hand during those 15 minutes? What is the distance the tip has traveled along the straight line between its two positions?

</Exercise>

</Exercise>

<Exercise id="rev-working-backwards" title="Working Backwards" solution="appendix">

Find a vector $\vv{v} \in \R^3$ such that $\norm{\vv{v}} = 6$ and $\vv{v} - 2\colvec{1\\0\\-1} = \colvec{0\\4\\2}$. (First solve for $\vv{v}$, then verify the norm condition.)

</Exercise>

<Exercise id="rev-convex" title="Convex Combinations" solution="appendix">

A linear combination $c_1\vv{v}_1 + c_2\vv{v}_2$ is called a **convex combination** if $c_1, c_2 \ge 0$ and $c_1 + c_2 = 1$. Let $\vv{v}_1 = \colvec{1\\0}$ and $\vv{v}_2 = \colvec{0\\3}$.

1. Describe geometrically the set of all convex combinations of $\vv{v}_1$ and $\vv{v}_2$.
2. Is $\colvec{1/2\\3/2}$ a convex combination of $\vv{v}_1$ and $\vv{v}_2$? Prove your answer.
3. Can a convex combination of $\vv{v}_1$ and $\vv{v}_2$ ever lie outside the line segment connecting them? Why or why not?

</Exercise>
