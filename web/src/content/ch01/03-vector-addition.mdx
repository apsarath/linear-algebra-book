Suppose you walk 3 blocks east and 2 blocks north, then continue 1 block east and 4 blocks north. Your total displacement is 4 blocks east and 6 blocks north. You just *added* two vectors.

### The geometric picture

There are two equivalent ways to visualize vector addition.

**Tip-to-tail rule.** To add $\vv{a}$ and $\vv{b}$, place the tail of $\vv{b}$ at the tip of $\vv{a}$. The sum $\vv{a} + \vv{b}$ is the arrow from the tail of $\vv{a}$ to the tip of $\vv{b}$.

<Figure src="ch01/tip-to-tail.svg" alt="Tip-to-tail addition: b starts where a ends, and a + b goes from the tail of a to the tip of b." />

**Parallelogram rule.** Draw both vectors from the same point. Complete the parallelogram. The diagonal from the common tail is $\vv{a} + \vv{b}$.

<Figure src="ch01/parallelogram.svg" alt="Parallelogram addition: a and b drawn from the same point; the diagonal of the parallelogram they span is a + b." />

Take a moment to convince yourself that both rules give the same result. (They'd better—otherwise we'd have two different definitions of addition!)

### The algebraic rule

In coordinates, addition is beautifully simple: just add corresponding components.

<Definition>

If $\vv{a} = \colvec{a_1\\a_2}$ and $\vv{b} = \colvec{b_1\\b_2}$, then

$$
\vv{a} + \vv{b} = \colvec{a_1 + b_1 \\ a_2 + b_2}.
$$

The same rule applies component-wise in $\R^n$ for any $n$.

</Definition>

<Example id="ex-vector-addition">

Let $\vv{a} = \colvec{3\\-1}$ and $\vv{b} = \colvec{2\\5}$. Then

$$
\vv{a} + \vv{b} = \colvec{3+2\\-1+5} = \colvec{5\\4}.
$$

</Example>

### Properties of addition

You might guess that vector addition inherits nice properties from ordinary addition of real numbers. Let's verify a couple.

*Is addition commutative?* That is, does $\vv{a} + \vv{b} = \vv{b} + \vv{a}$? In components:

$$
\colvec{a_1 + b_1 \\ a_2 + b_2} = \colvec{b_1 + a_1 \\ b_2 + a_2},
$$

which is true because addition of real numbers is commutative. Geometrically, the parallelogram rule makes this obvious: the diagonal doesn't change if you swap the two sides.

*Is addition associative?* Yes: $(\vv{a} + \vv{b}) + \vv{c} = \vv{a} + (\vv{b} + \vv{c})$, again because real-number addition is associative. We can therefore write $\vv{a} + \vv{b} + \vv{c}$ without ambiguity.

<Exercise>

Compute $\vv{u} + \vv{v}$ where $\vv{u} = \colvec{1\\-3\\4}$ and $\vv{v} = \colvec{-2\\7\\0}$.

</Exercise>

<Solution>

$\vv{u} + \vv{v} = \colvec{1+(-2)\\-3+7\\4+0} = \colvec{-1\\4\\4}$.

</Solution>

<Exercise>

Verify, by drawing a picture in $\R^2$, that the tip-to-tail rule and the parallelogram rule agree for $\vv{a} = \colvec{2\\1}$ and $\vv{b} = \colvec{1\\3}$.

</Exercise>

<Solution>

$\vv{a} + \vv{b} = \colvec{3\\4}$. Both constructions reach the same point:

<Figure src="ch01/sol-addition-rules.svg" alt="Side by side: tip-to-tail and parallelogram constructions for a = (2,1) and b = (1,3), both ending at (3,4)." />

Both give $\vv{a}+\vv{b} = \colvec{3\\4}$. ✓

</Solution>
